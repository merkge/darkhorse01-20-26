## The Fallacy of Objective Science & the Primacy of Gnosis

1: The Fallacy of Objective Science 

Modern science and secular philosophy operate under a fundamental, unexamined dogmatism: the belief that true objective knowledge must be gathered from an outside world via detached empirical observation. But this premise collapses once we examine the nature of consciousness itself. As Max Planck noted, consciousness is fundamental; science cannot "get behind" it because every observation presupposes a conscious observer. This was further dismantled by physical chemist and philosopher Michael Polanyi, who in 1958 demonstrated that the ideal of strictly detached objectivity is a myth, as all formal knowledge rests on a foundation of direct, tacit personal awareness. Science can never achieve an "outside" perspective on consciousness because no such perspective exists.

​2: Kant, Jung, and the Numinous

To understand the limits of standard empiricism, we must look to Immanuel Kant. Kant demonstrated that our everyday minds do not perceive an external world raw and unfiltered. Rather, we intuitively "dress up" the underlying reality—what he called the noumenon, or the thing-in-itself—by filtering it through the human "transcendental categories" of space, time, and causality, resulting in our perceived phenomena. Carl Jung took this insight to its ultimate depth, showing that these hidden filters are living archetypal structures within a shared, collective unconscious—structures that confront consciousness with what Jung called the numinous: an immediate, awe-inspiring experience of the transcendent. Kant’s error was assuming that ordinary discursive reason is the absolute boundary of human consciousness, concluding that we are permanently barred from direct access to the noumenal or ultimate reality.

​ 3. The Origin (1997 - Rothko Chapel & The Affirmation of Unity)

This trajectory was initiated in 1997 at the Rothko Chapel, where I encountered Lex Hixon’s Atom from the Sun of Knowledge. Returning daily to sit in contemplation with its opening prayer—an affirmation of Ibn 'Arabi’s Wahdat al-Wujud (the Unity of Being)—I anchored my silent mantra, La ilaha illallah, in its central invocation: "May we be guided through every experience along the direct path of love that leads from the human heart into the divine heart." Each time I sat with this prayer, my breathing was completely taken over—air came rushing in and out beyond my conscious control, leaving me suspended as if on a magic carpet. Though I questioned at the time whether it was of God or something else, the experience felt so undeniably extraordinary and deeply good that there was no way I wasn't coming back to do it again the next day—initiating years of profound internal integration.

​Conclusion (Dismantling Kant)

This exposes the ultimate blind spot of standard empiricism: it assumes that objective reality exists only "out there" in the physical world, treating the interior mind as a mere subjective shadow. But when the rational ego is bypassed—whether through ideomotor dynamics, past-memory retrieval, or direct spiritual illumination—the infinite inner world reveals itself. The sacred stories and revelations across all holy books are not merely ancient history; they are timeless dynamics actively repeating within the deepest strata of our shared consciousness. Direct gnosis is not an imagined projection, but the only truly objective knowledge we can possess—an undeniable, lived encounter with the infinite reality dwelling within us all.
